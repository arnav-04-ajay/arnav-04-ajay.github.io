Project Overview

My roommate and I were tired of having to use a key to lock and unlock our dorm door, since we were spoiled by our old dorm which automatically locked and used an RFID scan from our phone to unlock the door. However, we thought we could take this a step further, and design a system to lock and unlock the door based on our proximity to the dorm, thus making the whole process hands free.

Project Goals

Convenience

Wanted the whole system to be hands free and reliable.

Low Power Usage

Didn't want to have to constantly deal with recharging (and risk getting locked out if the system ran out of power).

Easy Installation

Wanted the system to be easy to set up and remove (we don't want to get fined for anything permanent on the door).

Physical Design

The physical design was simply a 3D printed housing for a servo motor and our circuit (which was on a protoboard). The full housing setup could be screwed onto existing screw holes placed on the door for the lock.

Circuitry

The simple protoboard circuit contained an ESP32 and a step-down power conversion board (to manage power coming from a power bank and operate the ESP32 and the servo motor).

Bluetooth Proximity Use

First, the users' phones are paired to the ESP32 via bluetooth by running a pairing script on the ESP32. Then, the ESP32 is configured as a BLE (Bluetooth Low Energy) server, which detects if other previously paired devices are within a certain BLE signal strength range, which then handles a simple state system to turn the servo motor which either unlocks or locks the door (depending on if paired users are or aren't within range respectively). Manual overrides can also be sent to the ESP32 via BLE communication from the users' phones.
